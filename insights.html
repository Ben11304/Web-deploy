04Regional signature

Each region owns a different hazard profile.

Radar of five hazards over the four Census regions. The Northeast is hurricane- and winter-dominated; the West, wildfire; the South carries the hurricane load with little winter exposure.

05What moves with what

Three correlations stand out. Most do not.

State-level Pearson r, n=50. Significance at p<0.05. Flood and hurricane exposure co-locate tightly on the coasts (r=0.75). Multi-hazard load tracks power outages (r=0.46). Single-hazard outage signals, including earthquake and hurricane, are noisier than usually reported.

06Social amplification

Multi-hazard burden tracks social vulnerability.

Hazard exposure pooled with CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 at the county level. Multi-hazard exposure shows the strongest coupling (r=0.51, p<0.001) — counties that already score high on social vulnerability carry a disproportionate share of the multi-hazard load.

      09Threshold sensitivity

      Move the earthquake threshold and watch the picture swing.

      Reported figures use PGA ≥ 0.092g (MMI ≥ VI, IEEE 693-qualification floor). Lowering the threshold to the USGS any-perception floor (0.014g) pushes earthquake exposure to 97% — a reminder that headline numbers are meaningful only paired with their threshold.
