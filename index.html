Multi-Hazard Exposure Assessment · v3-PSHA-FEMA · 2026-06-04

How exposed is the U.S. energy grid to natural hazards?

A reproducible, open-data assessment of 2.83 million energy assets against five modeled hazards — earthquake, flood, wildfire, hurricane, and winter storm — joined with social vulnerability and observed power-outage records. Built for policy makers, emergency planners, and researchers.

Approach · 00

A data-driven framework, fully traceable to source

The assessment integrates federal hazard models, social vulnerability indices, and observed impact data — producing per-asset exposure classifications and county-level risk surfaces. Every threshold cites a U.S. federal agency or peer-reviewed paper; nothing is inferred, simulated, or averaged across sources. Headline numbers report the non-tower primary stratum (n=205,783); all-assets sensitivity (n=2,827,591) is reported in parallel.

Findings · 02

Three signals from the data

Per-hazard social vulnerability associations, network exposure, and outage relationships. All p-values evaluated against a Bonferroni-corrected family of m=87 tests (cutoff α/87 = 5.7×10⁻⁴).

Five hazards, five signals
SVI × hazard exposure (per-hazard OR)
Disaggregating by hazard reveals heterogeneous direction. Hurricane is the strongest signal but carries a known coastal Southeast / Black Belt structural confound.

Network vulnerability × exposure
Of HIGH-vulnerability nodes (top-25%-minus-CRITICAL stratum by betweenness centrality), 72.15% are exposed to ≥1 modeled hazard. Permutation null mean 67.84%, z=17.63, p≈0 — directional signal is significant; effect-size lift +4.31 pp is small because the 5H baseline is already high.
